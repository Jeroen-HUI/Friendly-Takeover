Real takeovers, bad bets and missed deals. Each one is a two-minute read, and each one is a move you can try on your friends.

How Nokia lost the phone market in six years

In 2007 Nokia was the world's biggest phone maker. In 2013 it sold its phone business to Microsoft for about $7.2 billion. What happened in between, and the one bet that did it.

The day Blockbuster turned down Netflix for $50 million

In 2000 Netflix offered to sell itself to Blockbuster for $50 million. Blockbuster said no. Ten years later Blockbuster was bankrupt. The story, and why big companies miss small ones.

Nintendo made playing cards for 88 years before its first console

Nintendo was founded in 1889 in Kyoto as a maker of hand-made playing cards. Its first video game console came out in 1977. What a 137-year-old company teaches about changing business.

Coca-Cola could have bought Pepsi three times

Pepsi went bankrupt in 1923, and between 1922 and 1933 Coca-Cola was offered the company three times. It said no each time. Today PepsiCo has higher revenue than Coca-Cola.

Red Bull doesn't make Red Bull

Every can of Red Bull is produced by another company, the Austrian family firm Rauch. Red Bull itself does the marketing. How a company sells billions of cans without a factory.

Lamborghini started with a complaint about a Ferrari

Ferruccio Lamborghini built tractors and drove Ferraris. The story goes that a complaint about a clutch led him to start his own car company in 1963, to beat Ferrari.

Why nobody can buy IKEA

Most IKEA stores belong to a group owned by a Dutch foundation, the Stichting INGKA Foundation. A foundation has no shareholders, which makes IKEA close to takeover-proof.
