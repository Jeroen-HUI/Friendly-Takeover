A business simulation game for students

Friendly Takeovers was built by a business student who did a simulation in class and wanted a version his friends would play voluntarily. Same kind of decisions, in rounds, in one shared market, with a lot more at stake socially.

What students practise

TopicIn the game
Pricing and demandSet a price in a market where buyer groups differ in how much they care about price, quality and brand.
Capacity planningDecide production volume; overtime and idle capacity both cost money.
PurchasingBuy raw materials a round ahead, with a price outlook that is usually but not always right.
Reading reportsProfit and loss, cash flow, balance sheet and cost per unit, every round.
ForecastingAn investor call each round: forecast sales and profit, and get rewarded for accuracy.
FinanceCredit lines, interest, dividends and what they do to company value.
Corporate controlShares, takeover bids, friendly mergers and board votes weighted by shareholding.

As in a course simulation, nobody tells you how much you will sell. There is a market report, a calculator and a notepad. The rest is up to the team.

Fits in a lesson

The host chooses the number of rounds and the minutes per round. A Blitz game is five rounds of two and a half minutes, short enough to play and discuss in one session. Longer set-ups work for a project week. A guided first round walks new players through a real forecast.

For teachers

You can try the whole thing alone against bots first, for free. The host can switch individual mechanics on or off, such as takeovers, mergers, dividends and auctions, and give players handicaps. Questions people ask

Is this a replacement for TOPSIM or another course simulation?

No. It is a game, not a graded course tool, and it is not affiliated with TOPSIM or any other simulation provider. It covers the same kind of decisions in a lighter form, which makes it useful as a warm-up, an icebreaker or practice outside class.

How many students can play at once?

Up to 8 companies per game, and you can run as many games side by side as you like. Students can also play in teams, with one team sharing a company on one screen.

Does it need installing on school computers?

No. It runs in the browser on laptops, tablets and phones. Students join with a 6-character code or a link.

Do students need an account?

Playing against bots needs no account at all. Online games use a guest profile or a free account with an email address.

Is Friendly Takeovers free?

Playing solo against bots is free, and so is your first online game with friends. After that the full game is a one-time purchase of €8.99, with unlimited online games. There is no subscription and there are no in-game purchases.
