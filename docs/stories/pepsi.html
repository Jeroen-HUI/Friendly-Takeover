Coca-Cola could have bought Pepsi three times

Coca-Cola could have bought Pepsi. Three times. It said no every time.

What happened

Pepsi-Cola was invented in the 1890s by Caleb Bradham, a pharmacist in North Carolina. After the First World War he bet heavily on sugar prices and lost. In 1923 Pepsi went bankrupt. It went under again in 1931.

Between 1922 and 1933, Pepsi was offered to the Coca-Cola Company three times. Coca-Cola declined each time. From where it stood, that made sense: why pay for a failing copy of your own product?

Pepsi's next owners tried something different. During the Depression they sold a bottle twice the size for the same five cents. Sales took off, and Coca-Cola had a rival for life.

Today PepsiCo, which also owns snack brands such as Lay's and Doritos, brings in more revenue than Coca-Cola: about $92 billion against about $47 billion in 2024. Coca-Cola is still the more profitable and more valuable company, but it has spent a century and a great deal of money fighting a competitor it could have bought for very little.

The lesson

The deal you skip can become your biggest rival. A struggling competitor is cheap for a reason, and sometimes the reason is temporary.

In Friendly Takeovers

When a rival has a terrible round, their share price drops and they need cash. That is the moment to make an offer. Wait two rounds and they may have recovered, and remember that you did not help.
