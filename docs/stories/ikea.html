Why nobody can buy IKEA

The world's biggest furniture retailer is owned by a Dutch foundation. That makes it close to impossible to take over.

How it works

IKEA was founded in Sweden in 1943 by Ingvar Kamprad. In the 1980s he moved ownership out of the family's hands. Today most IKEA stores are run by Ingka Group, and Ingka Group is owned by the Stichting INGKA Foundation, registered in the Netherlands. The IKEA brand and concept sit in a separate group, Inter IKEA.

A foundation has no shareholders. There are no shares on a stock exchange, no family members who can sell their part, and nobody to make an offer to. A hostile takeover needs someone to buy from. Here there is nobody.

In 2006, The Economist looked at the structure and called the foundation the world's richest charity, and one of the least generous, since at the time little of the money went to charitable work. The foundation's giving has grown considerably since then.

The lesson

Who owns a company matters as much as how well it sells. Kamprad wanted IKEA to stay independent and to think in decades, not quarters, and he designed the ownership to make that the only option.

Source: The Economist, May 2006.
