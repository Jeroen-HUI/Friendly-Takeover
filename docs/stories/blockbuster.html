The day Blockbuster turned down Netflix for $50 million

In 2000, Netflix offered to sell itself to Blockbuster for $50 million. Blockbuster laughed them out of the room.

What happened

Netflix was then a small company posting DVDs to people's homes, and it was losing money. Its founders flew to Dallas to meet Blockbuster's management with a proposal: buy us for $50 million and we will run your online business. As Netflix co-founder Marc Randolph tells it, the Blockbuster side could barely keep a straight face.

It is easy to see why. At its peak a few years later, Blockbuster had about 9,000 stores. It was the place where people rented films. A mail-order DVD service looked like a rounding error.

Then the stores became the problem. Rent, staff and late fees that customers hated were hard to walk away from, because they were also where the money came from. Blockbuster filed for bankruptcy in 2010. Today there is one Blockbuster left, in Bend, Oregon. Netflix is worth hundreds of billions of dollars.

The lesson

The thing that makes a company big is often the thing that stops it from changing. And the price of a small rival is never lower than on the day they come to you asking.
