How Nokia lost the phone market in six years

In 2007, Nokia was the biggest phone maker on Earth. Six years later its phone business was sold, and two years after that the buyer wrote almost all of it off.

What happened

In 2007 Apple launched the iPhone, and in 2008 the first Android phones arrived. Nokia's own software, Symbian, was built for an older kind of phone and struggled to keep up.

In February 2011 Nokia had to pick what came next. It could have joined Android, like most other phone makers. Instead it bet everything on Microsoft's Windows Phone. The reasoning was that on Android, Nokia would be one maker among many, while on Windows Phone it would be the main partner.

It flopped. Buyers and app developers had already chosen sides. In September 2013 Microsoft agreed to buy Nokia's phone business for about $7.2 billion. In 2015 Microsoft wrote down about $7.6 billion on the deal, which is more or less all of it.

The lesson

Nokia did not lose because it made bad phones. It lost because of one strategic bet, made when it still had options. Being the biggest protects you for a while. It does not protect you from picking the wrong side.

Nokia itself survived, by the way. It is now a network equipment company.
