Nintendo made playing cards for 88 years before its first console

Nintendo is 137 years old. It started in 1889, in Kyoto, making hand-made playing cards.

What happened

The company was founded by Fusajiro Yamauchi to make hanafuda, traditional Japanese flower cards. For decades, that was the business. In 1959 Nintendo signed a deal to put Disney characters on its cards, which opened up a new market: children.

In the 1960s the card market stopped growing and Nintendo started looking for something else. It moved into toys, and from toys into electronic games. Its first home console, the Color TV-Game, came out in Japan in 1977. That is 88 years after the company was founded.

The rest is better known: Donkey Kong in 1981, the Famicom in 1983, the Game Boy in 1989, and so on to the Switch.

The lesson

The business you start is not always the one you become. Nintendo did not jump from cards to consoles in one move. It went from cards to children's cards, to toys, to electronic toys, to video games. Each step was small and next door to the last one.
