How to play Friendly Takeovers

You run one company for a set number of rounds against your rivals. Whoever's company is worth the most at the end wins, unless someone takes control of every other company first. That is a monopoly, and it ends the game on the spot.

A round, step by step

1. Watch the news

Each round starts with broadcasts: news that changes demand, costs or interest for this round. Rare mega events, such as a crash or a boom, hit everyone.

2. Read

The Market tab has the market report, the history and what your rivals did. Reports has your profit, cash, balance sheet, production and cost per unit. A paid market study shows the split per buyer group.

3. Decide

Go through the tabs from top to bottom: Production (price, amount, quality), R&D, Marketing, Retail, Finance, Takeovers and the Boardroom. A tick shows which tabs you have visited this round.

4. Submit

You see your decision sheet first. Then everyone's decisions are resolved together and the results come in.

What decides your sales

Buyers compare every company on price, quality, brand and whether the product is actually on the shelf. Different buyer groups weigh those differently, and those groups slowly grow or shrink during a game. Nobody tells you how much you will sell. You have a calculator, a notepad and last round's numbers.

Money

Raw materials are bought a round ahead at today's price. If you run short, the game buys the rest on the spot market at 15% extra. Leftovers cost a little to store.

The factory has a sweet spot. Too full and you pay overtime, too empty and idle machines cost money.

Loans come from the bank up to your credit line. Run out of cash and you get an expensive emergency loan.

The investor call lets you forecast your sales and profit each round. Being close raises investor trust, which raises your value and makes loans cheaper. Being far off lowers it.

Shares and takeovers

From round 3 the exchange is open during the round. Every purchase moves the price and shows on the ticker for everyone. Owning 40% of a company without controlling it makes headlines and warns the target. Past 50% you take control through a takeover bid when the round ends.

You can also do it by handshake. In the chat you can sell or buy shares for an agreed price, lend money, sign non-attack pacts for one to four rounds, or propose a friendly merger where two companies become one.

Seven tips for your first game

  1. Do not start a price war in round 1. The cheapest company sells the most units and often earns the least.
  2. Produce what you can sell. A full warehouse is money standing still. Check last round's demand before you set production.
  3. Buy materials when the outlook says prices will rise. The outlook is right about three times out of four.
  4. Take the investor call seriously. A careful forecast is the cheapest value you can add.
  5. Keep some credit free. You need it the round someone starts buying your shares.
  6. Watch the ticker from round 3. If one name keeps buying your shares, find a friend to sign a pact with, or buy back.
  7. Remember your secret mission. The bonus is often the difference between second and first.

Questions people ask

How do you win Friendly Takeovers?

Have the most valuable company when the last round ends, or take control of every other company before then.

How is company value calculated?

Cash minus debt, plus factory, stores, stock, brand, earnings power and the shares you hold in rival companies.

When can you buy shares in other companies?

From round 3, when the stock exchange opens. You can buy a limited number of shares in each company per round, and taking control past 50% goes through a takeover bid at the end of the round.

How many people can play?

Up to 8 players in one game. If you are short of people, the host can add up to 4 computer-run companies, and you can play alone against bots.

Is Friendly Takeovers free?

Playing solo against bots is free, and so is your first online game with friends. After that the full game is a one-time purchase of €8.99, with unlimited online games. There is no subscription and there are no in-game purchases.
